The proposal acknowledges acceptance before durable storage. A process crash between “sent” and the Kafka write loses an accepted message. A volatile outage buffer cannot satisfy the promise, and dropping its oldest entries explicitly destroys already accepted work. Redis Pub/Sub also cannot be the recovery source: subscribers can miss messages during disconnects or failures. Finally, making delivery wait for a five-second Cassandra batch contradicts a sub-500-ms online delivery target.

Repair the acceptance boundary first. Give each send a stable client message ID and acknowledge “accepted” only after a durable append succeeds. If retaining Kafka, configure producer acknowledgments, replication, and minimum in-sync replicas to match the failure assumptions. Deduplicate client retries and consumer processing by message ID. When Kafka is unavailable, return retryable failure or apply bounded backpressure before accepting; do not acknowledge volatile buffering as durable acceptance. A local durable spool is another option, but adds recovery machinery and is unnecessary for the minimal repair.

Use the durable stream to trigger immediate online delivery. Remove Cassandra's five-second batch from the delivery critical path. Cassandra may remain an asynchronous projection for history if Kafka retention safely covers projection outages and replay, and consumer offsets are committed only after the relevant downstream work can be recovered. If Cassandra must be authoritative before delivery, write promptly instead of waiting for that batch.

Redis Pub/Sub can remain a low-latency routing hint, provided lost notifications are repaired by a durable consumer/replay path. Keep pending delivery state or reconstruct it from the durable log and device cursors; retries after worker failure must not depend on the vanished Redis notification. Client ACKs record device receipt, distinct from server acceptance. Clients deduplicate repeated deliveries.

Specify the latency promise as a measurable percentile and operating condition, including connected reachable recipients and dependency health. No design can guarantee delivery under 500 ms through arbitrary network partitions. Measure the complete sender-to-recipient path, including durable append, consumer lag, routing, and transport. Normal operation can fit the target with immediate consumption; durability should not be bypassed to hide an outage.

Test process crashes immediately before and after each acceptance boundary, Kafka outages with buffer pressure, missing Pub/Sub notifications, slow Cassandra writes, and duplicate sends/consumer replay. Accepted messages must remain recoverable, and healthy-path latency must meet the stated percentile under peak load.
